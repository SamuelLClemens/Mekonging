The old model — one flat `km * 1.35 / 50 kmh`, i.e. 1.62 min per straight-line km — measured **23–63% too fast on short journeys and 18–30% too slow on long ones**. Minutes per straight-line km is not constant: it falls with distance, and in Thailand it rises first through the 12–90 km band that is all provincial roads and market towns before the motorways take over.
  Four filters were needed before the fit meant anything, each found by inspecting outliers rather than assumed: a road/straight ratio outside 0.95–2.5 (set to retain Pai → Chiang Mai at 2.35× while excluding barrier detours); an implied average speed outside 25–110 km/h, which is the one that actually catches ferries — Maya Bay → Koh Kradan passes the detour test at 2.23× but implies 18 km/h for eight hours, which is a boat; collapsing place pairs to **corridors** before taking quantiles, because eleven curated places around Krabi share two roads and drowned out the single Bangkok–Ayutthaya motorway pair; and scaling the corridor cell to a quarter of each band's own length, after a fixed ~55 km cell merged both ends of every short pair into one cell and silently made four bands inherit their neighbour's figures.

Scored against six known journeys, 4 of 6 fall inside the range; both misses (Bangkok → Ayutthaya, Phnom Penh → Kampong Cham) are fast trunk roads in regions whose median is much slower, and both were wrong under the old model too. `NEAR_MAX_MIN` 75 → 90: the cap was held down because a straight-line guess put Pai → Chiang Mai at ~89 min, and the calibrated curve puts it at 126, comfortably outside. ## Priority 8 — The emergency screen's speed, and colour that can actually be read (mk-v0.464.0)
- [x] **8.1** `allCare()` in `js/data/hospitals.js` was quadratic, and it is the emergency screen's hot path. Every OpenStreetMap row was compared against every row already kept — about six million name-and-distance comparisons for Thailand's 3,693 rows — and `hospitalScreen()` calls into it up to ten times per render (nearest hospitals, then all care, then `nearestAnywhere` twice, each of those across four countries). Measured before, on a laptop: `allCare('th')` **1,540 ms**, `allCare('vi')` 754 ms, `nearestAnywhere()` across all four **2,125 ms**, and `#hospital` **990 ms to first paint / 2,000 ms to settle** against 32–41 ms for `transport-th`, the largest content screen in the app. It was the slowest screen by two orders of magnitude and it is the one somebody opens when they are hurt; a mid-range phone is several times slower again.
  Three changes, none of which alters a row of output: the finished list is memoised per country against the rows array the loader stored; the kept rows are bucketed into a 0.01° grid (at least 1.0 km on both axes in this region, so the 3×3 neighbourhood is a complete candidate set for a test that can only match inside 300 m) with candidates tried in insertion order so the FIRST match still wins; and `distinctive()` is memoised, because the handful of curated names were being re-tokenised once per OSM row.
